# Design: how the Bi³ data, metrics and trajectory generation are defined

This document states how each key quantity in this repository is defined, where the definition comes from, and which constant or function holds it. Sources are of three kinds, tagged on each entry:
- **Bi³ paper**: Stratton et al., ICRA 2026 ([arXiv 2605.06863](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06863)), with the section.
- **Measured**: our measurement on the Bi³ data, with the number.
- **Design choice**: a choice made for this study, with its reason.

Code references are `path:symbol`. Where a measurement and the code differ, the code is what runs. Usage (commands, output files) is in the [README](README.md).

## 1. Dataset understanding and handling
These definitions are shared by every study in the repository.

### Bi³ basics
- **Two sites, one robot each.** LAAS-CNRS (Toulouse, France) with a PR2, footprint 0.668 x 0.668 m, and the University of Michigan (UM, Ann Arbor, USA) with a Hello Robot Stretch, footprint 0.330 x 0.340 m. *Source:* Bi³ paper, Sec. I and III-D. *Code:* `preprocessing/extract_trips.py:SITE_FRAMES` (`robot`, `footprint_m`).
- **Five controllers.** All plug into the same MPPI-based model-predictive controller and differ in how they treat people: no prediction (the robot does not perceive people), static obstacles, constant-velocity obstacles, Human Scene Transformer prediction, and CoHAN joint planning. *Source:* Bi³ paper, Sec. III-B to III-D. *Code:* `preprocessing/extract_trips.py:CONTROLLERS` uses the dataset's file names `blind`, `static`, `cv`, `hst`, `cohan`. The paper calls `blind` "NP" and `static` "ST".
- **A trial is one participant pair that ran all five controllers.** Two participants build towers by carrying blocks one at a time from piles to assembly stations along the room's perimeter, while the robot drives a random sequence of workstations that crosses the room between goals. Each pair runs all five controllers in a balanced Latin-square order, so a trial holds five recordings. There are 20 trials at LAAS and 17 at UM, 185 recordings in total. *Source:* Bi³ paper, Sec. IV-A, IV-B and V. A "recording" in this repository is one trial and controller.
- **Train/validation/test split.** Bi³'s test trials are `laas18`-`laas20` and `um16`, `um17`. *Source:* the Bi³ authors' code (fluentrobotics/Bi3, `create_data_npys.py`), not the paper. *Code:* `preprocessing/extract_trips.py:TEST_TRIALS`. The VLM planners are not trained on Bi³, so the split matters only for comparisons with learned models. The default trials for robot trips are `laas18` and `um13` (`DEFAULT_TRIALS`), because the UM test trials have no overhead video and the camera-input experiments need one.

### Room-centred frame and the LAAS rotation
- **Definition.** Every track and station is expressed in a room frame whose origin is the centre of the area people and the robot actually occupy, with LAAS rotated 180° so that both sites have their shared table on the +x wall. Origins in the mocap frame: LAAS (4.88, 15.62) m, UM (-0.23, 0.01) m. *Source:* design choice, so that one prompt layout and one map layout describe both sites. *Code:* `preprocessing/extract_trips.py:SITE_FRAMES`, `to_room`, `to_room_theta`.
- **Limitation.** The origin is set by hand from the occupied area, not from a surveyed room corner. The paper does not state the mocap frame.

### Traversable workspace, 3.5 x 3.5 m
- **Definition.** The room is the square of side 3.5 m around the room-frame origin. It is drawn on every map and is the base of the out-of-room check (Section 2). *Source:* Bi³ paper, Sec. IV-A ("traversable workspace"). *Code:* `preprocessing/extract_trips.py:SITE_FRAMES` (`room_m`).
- **Limitation.** The workstations stand along the perimeter, so people working at them stand at or just beyond its edge (Section 2, room bounds).

### Stations: robot goal points and people's points
- **Robot goal points.** Each site has six stations, numbered 0-5. A station's robot point is one of the six values the recorded robot goal takes at that site. *Source:* measured on the raw `robot_goal` field (Bi³'s own goal coordinates). *Code:* `preprocessing/annotate_goals.py:STATIONS`, written into `stations.json` and, in the room frame, as `xy` in `site_frames.json` (`preprocessing/extract_trips.py:site_frames`).
- **People's points.** A station's people's point is the median position at the turnaround (`t_turn`) over all non-inferred person visits with that label, over every recording of the site. Inferred visits are left out because their `t_turn` is the far point of a missed loop, not a turnaround (including them moves no median by more than 0.019 m). A shared table's point is the midpoint of its two stations' points. *Source:* measured. People's points lie 0.14-0.52 m from the robot points, toward the wall, and are stable across trials (the per-trial median lies a median 0.06-0.13 m from the site point, at most 0.32 m). *Code:* `preprocessing/extract_trips.py:human_goal_points`, stored as `human_xy` in `site_frames.json`. Test trials are included in the medians, since these are fixed station positions, not a fitted parameter.
- **Which point an agent uses.** A planned robot is given and scored against the robot point, a planned person against the people's point. *Code:* `traj_gen/agents.py:STATION_POSITION`. A person trip's `goal_xy` is its goal's `human_xy`.

### Shared tables
- **Definition.** One table holds two stations at each site: LAAS 0 and 1, UM 4 and 5. *Source:* measured: the station positions, and the camera frames show a real shared table at both sites. The paper does not describe the tables. *Code:* `preprocessing/annotate_goals.py:SHARED_TABLES`.
- **Consequence for labels.** Where people stand at a shared table (about 0.35 m apart at LAAS), position alone cannot tell the two stations apart. Visit detection resolves this by tower phase (below), and unresolved visits get the pair label `"0/1"` or `"4/5"`.

### Tables are not modelled as obstacles
- **Definition.** No table or furniture geometry enters the metrics. Stations are points, and the boxes on the maps mark where people go, not furniture. *Source:* design choice. The data give a station only as the robot's stopping point and the people's standing positions, so a table outline would be invented, and a free-space map built from the tracks adds machinery the study does not need. This is also why station crossing is not scored (Section 2).
- **Prompt guidance kept.** The robot's system prompt says that nobody passes through a station or leaves the room. It stays as general guidance not to hug the perimeter or pass through stations, without relying on exact station geometry. It is not scored. *Source:* design choice.

### Visit detection and goal labels
- **Visit.** A person's visit to a station is a turnaround of at least 100° (direction change between the 0.4 m of path before and after a sample) within 1.0 m of a station, located at the closest approach of the turning run. Turnarounds at one station less than 2 s apart merge. The visit spans the contiguous run of samples within the visit's own closest approach plus 0.15 m (`t_arrive` to `t_leave`). *Source:* design choice, because participants shuttle blocks between stations and Bi³ records only the robot's goals. *Code:* `preprocessing/annotate_goals.py:detect_visits`, `TURN_MIN_DEG`, `TURN_LOOK_M`, `VISIT_RADIUS_M`, `MERGE_S`, `AT_STATION_SLACK_M`.
- **Inferred visits.** A wide U-turn can stay under 100°. When someone goes between two visits at the same or neighbouring stations (at most 1 m apart) via a point more than 1 m from both, that far point is filled in as a visit marked `inferred`. *Code:* `detect_visits`, `LOOP_M`.
- **Shared-table correction.** Each person's sequence is split into their two tower phases. A phase's visits to a shared table take the phase's margin-weighted majority station when it holds at least 70% of the vote (marked `corrected`), and keep the pair label otherwise. This resolves every UM phase but about 75% of LAAS phases. About 7% of LAAS person visits are `"0/1"`, and no UM visit needed a pair. *Code:* `correct_by_phase`, `PHASE_MIN_SHARE`.
- **Goal labels.** A backward pass gives every sample the station the person is at or heading to (`goal`) and the one after (`next_goal`). Labels are text: a station `"0"`-`"5"` or a pair. *Code:* `label_from_visits`. The robot's labels come from its recorded goals (`robot_labels`).
- **Check against ground truth.** The same detector run on the robot finds 96% of its recorded goals, and 90% of its detections match one. *Source:* measured (`robot_check`). Participants have a median of 40 visits per recording at both sites.
- **Limitation.** The 1.0 m radius only gates where a turnaround can be detected. The at-station zone is per visit (closest approach plus 0.15 m), and people are a median 0.61 m (LAAS) and 0.43 m (UM) from the robot point at `t_arrive`.

### Video sync
- **Definition.** Each overhead video's time offset from its mocap is the lag that maximises the cross-correlation of frame-to-frame pixel change with the agents' summed mocap speed, both at 10 Hz, searched within ±20 s: `video_time = mocap_time + offset_s`. *Source:* design choice. *Measured:* over all 139 videos the offsets range from -1.7 to +0.2 s, median about -1 s at both sites. *Code:* `preprocessing/sync_video.py`, read with `preprocessing/bi3_derived.py:video_time`.

### Trip cutting
- **Robot trip.** From one goal switch of the robot to the next. The first trip of a recording starts at the recording start, and the last goal is never reached, so it gives no trip. *Code:* `preprocessing/extract_trips.py:robot_trips`.
- **Person trip.** From the person leaving visit k (`t_leave`, where their goal label switches to the next station) to the turnaround of visit k + 1 (`t_turn`). Ending at the turnaround rather than at `t_arrive` gives the replay baseline a real path that reaches where people stand: at `t_arrive` the real path is still a median 0.61 m (LAAS) and 0.46 m (UM) from the people's point (measured). *Code:* `human_trips`.
- **Anchored 0.4 s grid.** Each trip is sampled at 2.5 Hz on a grid anchored at its start (`time` is 0 there), so the start state, the camera frame at the start and the real-path metrics share one time. The grid runs from up to 2.8 s before the start (history, whole 0.4 s steps of the 3 s) to up to 15 s after the end (replay padding, so a slower generated agent still has the others to be scored against). `start_index` and `end_index` mark the first and last sample of the real trip. `end_index` is the scored sample nearest the real end (the later one on a tie, and never past the padding), so the scored end is at most 0.2 s from the real end. *Source:* design choice (a project decision to anchor at the goal switch). *Code:* `trip_span`, `preprocessing/bi3_derived.py:scored_end_index`, `TRIP_HZ`, `TRIP_HISTORY_S`, `preprocessing/extract_trips.py:POST_S`.
- **Series and scored tracks.** Once per recording, every agent gets a series on a 10 Hz grid from the recording's first sample. Each sample is the mean of the raw samples (repeated samples dropped) with time in the centred 0.1 s bin around it, the heading their circular mean, with no further smoothing, so outside gaps no sample draws on a raw sample 0.05 s or more after its time. A bin with no raw sample takes the PCHIP interpolant through the raw samples from 0.4 s before to 0.4 s after its gap (heading unwrapped, interpolated and wrapped), and at a recording edge the nearest sample, so inside a gap a sample uses raw samples up to 0.4 s after the gap's end. A trip's start and end snap to the nearest grid sample (at most 0.05 s), and its scored tracks are every 4th series sample counted from the start, so the start is a scored sample. *Source:* user decision (2026-11-23). The earlier 0.5 s moving average came from station-visit detection (`annotate_goals.py`, which keeps it) and erased the shape of the last 0.4 s that the model inputs need. At UM people's positions stop updating for 0.5 to 1.9 s at times and jump a median 16 cm across such gaps, so holding the last sample would show a frozen person who then jumps. On artificial gaps cut from gap-free raw tracks, PCHIP through 0.4 s on each side beat linear filling and an unconstrained cubic spline overshot (measured). *Code:* `preprocessing/extract_trips.py:binned_track`, `recording_series`, `trip_span`, `INPUT_BIN_S`, `GAP_CONTEXT_S`; `preprocessing/bi3_derived.py:INPUT_HZ`, `SCORED_STEP`, `trip_arrays`, `load_trips`.
- **Storage.** `recordings.npz` holds each recording's grid and every agent's series, empty-bin flags and labels once. `trips.json` holds per-trip metadata with the trip's index range on its recording's grid (first sample with history, start, end, last sample with padding), and `load_trips` cuts the trip arrays from the recordings. *Code:* `recording_arrays`, `trip_arrays`.
- **Counts.** The person trips of all 37 trials number 14,292 (LAAS 7710, UM 6582). The default robot trips (`laas18`, `um13`) number 144.

### The move-cutting rule, dropped hops and duplicates
- **Move.** The part of a person's path that the human-similarity features describe runs from the trip start to where the path enters its goal station, by the visit annotation's own rule: of the samples within the closest approach to the goal's robot points plus 0.15 m, the first of the contiguous run that ends at the closest approach. The closest approach is searched only up to the first sample within the arrival radius, where an episode stops, so a generated path and the real path of the same trip are cut alike (a replay episode's move equals its trip's real move). A path that never gets within the arrival radius plus `GOAL_SLACK` has no move. *Source:* design choice, so both sets measure the same segment. *Code:* `metrics/traj_gen_metrics.py:move_end_index`.
- **Visit pairs that give no trip.** A pair is dropped when the next visit's `t_arrive` is not after this visit's `t_leave`: 17 LAAS hops between neighbouring stations whose at-station zones overlap (0.2% of LAAS pairs), and 83 LAAS and 26 UM duplicate same-station records. Kept pairs at the same station (LAAS 43, UM 8, loops away from a station and back) are also dropped, because goal-to-goal generation cannot represent them. *Source:* measured. *Code:* `preprocessing/extract_trips.py:human_trips`.
- **Limitation.** The real pool has no examples of the 17 neighbouring-station hops.

## 2. Metrics
All metrics score one executed path of the planned agent against the other agents, replayed from the log. They are exploratory definitions for this pilot, not a validated scoring against the Bi³ ratings.

### Agents as disks
- **Definition.** Every agent is a disk around its tracked centre. A planned path has no heading, so the robot's corners cannot be modelled. *Source:* design choice. *Code:* `metrics/traj_gen_metrics.py` module docstring, `traj_gen/agents.py`.

### Body (scoring) radii
- **Robot.** The footprint's inscribed disk, half the shorter side: PR2 0.334 m, Stretch 0.165 m. *Source:* design choice, the realistic body. *Code:* `traj_gen/agents.py:body_radius`.
- **Person.** 0.15 m, because mocap gives only the body centre. *Source:* design choice, the more generous of the radii considered. *Code:* `traj_gen/agents.py:PERSON_BODY_RADIUS_M`.
- **Measured basis.** At these radii, real controllers touch a person (clearance below 0) in 2 of the 16 robot test trips of the first robot-generation run, drawn from `laas18` and `um13` (clearances -0.008 and -0.018 m) and in 3 of the 133 robot trips with a full prompt history. Real people touch the robot in 0.44% (LAAS) and 0.33% (UM) of their moves. *Inferred:* these are unlikely to be real collisions, because both bodies are modelled as circles. *Code:* the counts are in the `metrics/traj_gen_metrics.py` docstring.

### Personal space
- **Definition.** A person has a personal space of radius 0.25 m around their centre, a 0.10 m buffer beyond the body radius. The robot has none. `personal_space_overlap` is the share of the episode's time steps with the planned agent's body inside some person's personal space. *Source:* design choice, not a measurement, with 0.20 m as the sensitivity value. Giving the robot a personal space would put a social notion on the robot that the study does not ask for. *Measured:* real people enter the other person's personal space in 0.1% (LAAS) and 0.2% (UM) of moves. *Code:* `traj_gen/agents.py:PERSONAL_SPACE_RADIUS_M`, `metrics/traj_gen_metrics.py:clearance_metrics`.

### Arrival radii and the scoring slack
- **Robot, 0.5 m.** The robot is told it has arrived within 0.5 m of its goal's robot point, and its episode ends there. *Source:* measured: Bi³'s recorded goal switches when the robot is within about 0.5 m of the goal. The paper does not state a switch rule. *Code:* `traj_gen/agents.py:ARRIVAL_RADIUS_M`.
- **Person, 0.4 m.** Around the goal's people's point. *Source:* measured. Real turnarounds lie a median 0.17 m (LAAS) and 0.16 m (UM) from the point, 90th percentile 0.36 and 0.30 m, so 0.4 m is about where people's own standing scatter ends. Real person trips come within 0.4 m of the goal in 91.1% (LAAS) and 89.5% (UM) of trips, and within the scored 0.55 m (0.4 m plus the 0.15 m slack) in 97.0% and 97.9% (of the 7,709 LAAS and 6,582 UM trips in trips_v2_human that start outside 0.4 m). *Code:* `traj_gen/agents.py:ARRIVAL_RADIUS_M`.
- **Slack, 0.15 m.** `goal_reached` counts a path that gets within the arrival radius plus 0.15 m (robot 0.65 m, person 0.55 m). *Source:* measured: the cleaned room-frame real robot paths end 0.48-0.60 m from the goal at their last sample before Bi³'s goal switch, at most 0.602 m over all 144 robot trips, and 0.15 m is a round value with margin above that. With it, every real controller reaches its goal. *Code:* `metrics/traj_gen_metrics.py:GOAL_SLACK`, `traj_gen_metrics`.

### One centre per station (multimodality)
- **Definition.** Each station has one centre per agent kind, and the arrival check is a disk around it. *Source:* design choice. *Measured:* per station, the radius holding 95% of real person arrivals is 0.37-0.52 m at LAAS and 0.32-0.42 m at UM. Arrivals spread more along the wall (95% half-width 0.23-0.50 m) than across it (0.16-0.36 m), with no sign of two separate clusters on the single check made (mean offset across the wall within 0.013 m of zero everywhere).
- **Limitation.** If arrivals cluster on separate sides of a station, a single centre overstates their spread and the arrival check becomes lenient. The check above is a summary number, not a histogram.

### Metric classes and the invalid rate
- **Classes.** Every metric belongs to one class. `invalid`: the plan breaks a hard rule of the task (an unusable reply is invalid too). `contact`: closeness to the other agents, a failure of the planner's reasoning rather than of a hard constraint. `efficiency`: how the agent moves. `similarity`: how person-like a generated person's moves are (person runs only, reported in `similarity.csv`). *Source:* design choice. *Code:* `metrics/traj_gen_metrics.py:METRIC_CLASS`.
- **Parts of the invalid rate.** An episode is invalid when its reply is unusable (status `invalid_reply`), the server failed (status `error`), its plan exceeds the speed cap in any step, it does not reach the goal, or it leaves the room. Each part and the total are shares of all episodes of the condition. An episode without a path counts only in its status part, and counts as goal not reached. The speed-cap part is NaN for the baselines, which have no model reply. *Code:* `traj_gen/analysis/analyze.py:scored`, `INVALID_PARTS`.

### Room bounds
- **Definition.** An agent is out of the room when its centre leaves the square of half side 1.75 m (robot) or 2.0 m (person) around the room centre: the 3.5 m workspace plus a margin of 0 m or 0.25 m. *Source:* measured for people: people stand closer to the walls than the robot's stopping points, and real people leave the 1.75 m square in 11.9% (LAAS) and 35.7% (UM) of moves but the 2.0 m square in only 0.03% and 0.41%. For the robot, the station positions and walls bound it. *Code:* `traj_gen/agents.py:ROOM_MARGIN_M`, applied in `traj_gen/harness/episodes.py` and `metrics/traj_gen_metrics.py:traj_gen_metrics` (`out_of_room`).

### Collisions on the executed path
- **Definition.** `clearance_min` is the smallest centre distance minus both body radii, over the other agents and time, so below 0 the bodies touch. `contact_time` is the share of time steps in contact, and `contact_events` counts separate contacts (a run of consecutive steps counts once). All are computed on the executed path only, not on a longer plan. *Source:* design choice: not all output modes plan a long horizon and then re-plan, so only executed motion is comparable. *Code:* `metrics/traj_gen_metrics.py:clearance_gap`, `clearance_metrics`.
- **Limitation.** A touch between two 0.4 s samples is missed.

### No station-crossing metric
- **Definition.** No metric checks whether an agent passes through a station. *Source:* design choice: the tables are not well defined in the data (Section 1), and real people cross the station boxes in 11.9% of LAAS moves. *Code:* `traj_gen/analysis/analyze.py:SKIP` skips the two former station-crossing metric names found in older run folders.

### Efficiency metrics
- **Definition.** `time_to_goal` (first sample within the arrival radius plus slack), `path_efficiency` (straight start-to-end distance over distance travelled), path length, and speed and acceleration statistics. Speed, acceleration and path efficiency follow the Bi³ paper's robot metrics. The paper defines only path efficiency precisely, so speed is reported as mean, median, max and standard deviation, and acceleration as the mean magnitude of the 2-D acceleration (with the tangential mean and the max). Bi³'s minimum distance (centre to centre) is reported too, in the contact class. *Source:* Bi³ paper, Sec. VI and Fig. 7. *Code:* `metrics/bi3_metrics.py`.

### Human-similarity measures
- **Features.** Per move: speed per 0.4 s step, the absolute turning angle between consecutive 0.8 s displacements, the minimum centre distance to the robot, and the minimum centre distance to the other person (one value per move). *Source:* THÖR (Rudenko et al., RA-L 2020) supplies the kinds of feature, not their definitions (a project decision). *Code:* `metrics/human_similarity.py:trajectory_features`, `FEATURE_NAMES`.
- **0.8 s turning window.** THÖR measures curvature over a 4 s segment, longer than a median Bi³ move (3.0 s), so the window is two 0.4 s steps. A turning angle is kept only when both displacements are at least 0.15 m: below that, the median absolute turn is close to the 90° of a random direction (measured: below 0.10 m at LAAS and 0.15 m at UM). The threshold removes about 6% (LAAS) and under 5% (UM) of 0.8 s displacements. A move needs 1.6 s to give any turning sample. *Code:* `metrics/human_similarity.py:TURN_WINDOW_STEPS`, `traj_gen/analysis/analyze.py:TURN_MIN_DISPLACEMENT_M`.
- **Wasserstein-1 distance (W1) per feature.** Two sets of moves are compared per feature by the W1 distance of their pooled samples, in the feature's units, with a bootstrap interval over moves resampled within (start station, goal station) strata. *Code:* `metrics/human_similarity.py:feature_distances`.
- **Reference drawn by trial.** Each W1 comes with a real-against-real reference: for every trial of the site that none of the generated moves comes from, 20 random subsets of its moves, each matched to the generated set's size and station mix (as far as the trial allows), are compared with the real set without that trial, and all subsets are pooled. The reference is then the distribution of W1 for one real pair's set of the generated set's size: it holds both how pairs differ and the noise of a set that size, as the generated W1 does. A generated set comes from a few trials, and a reference drawn independently across trials would leave out how much participant pairs differ from one another. The reference interval is the 2.5 and 97.5 percentiles over the pooled draws (or their min and max below 40 draws). The subsets come from their own seeded generator per trial, station pair and draw, so conditions compared on the same trips get the same reference. *Source:* design choice. *Measured:* at LAAS, a replay of one recording's real moves fell outside a reference of moves drawn independently of their trial (speed W1 0.086 m/s against a reference median of 0.015 m/s). *Measured:* with one subset per trial, the reference bands of two models on the same trips differed by up to 0.03 m (min distance to robot, LAAS median); pooled, they agree to about 0.003. *Code:* `feature_distances`, `MIN_PERCENTILE_DRAWS`, `traj_gen/analysis/analyze.py:REFERENCE_DRAWS_PER_TRIAL`.
- **Two comparisons.** Against the site pool (the site's real moves from trials other than the generated moves' trials) and against the same test trips' real moves. *Code:* `traj_gen/analysis/analyze.py:similarity`.
- **Pool exclusions.** The real pool is every person trip of the site in the run's trips folder, minus trips that start within the arrival radius and trips whose real path has no move. Generated episodes that are not `ok` or have no move are left out and counted. *Code:* `traj_gen/analysis/analyze.py:real_pool`, written to `similarity_pool.csv`.
- **Limitation.** The bootstrap interval sits above W1 when the generated set is close to the real set, so in that range the reference interval is the comparison that tells a real-like set from an unlike one.

## 3. Generation-specific handling

### The planned agent
- **Definition.** Each trip names the agent being generated (`trip["agent"]`: `robot` or `human<k>`). All other entries of `trip["tracks"]` are replayed from the log. A person is generated as one of the real participants, to that person's actual next station, open loop. *Source:* design choice. *Code:* `traj_gen/agents.py:agent_kind`, `traj_gen/harness/episodes.py:Episode`.

### What each generator is told
- **Robot.** Its own goal at the robot point, the station robot points, and each person's position, recent motion, goal and next goal. *Code:* `traj_gen/observation/scene.py:Scene.text`, `traj_gen/prompts/state/robot/scene.txt`, `state/other_person.txt`.
- **Person.** Their own goal and next goal at the people's points, the station points where people stand, and the others' positions, recent motion and goals: the other person's goal and next goal, and the robot's goal as a station name only (no robot point, since a person is given only the people's points). The map labels the other person with their name only, without goals. *Source:* the user's decision for the full pilot: this is the default for a planned person, not a separate condition, to keep the number of conditions small. *Code:* `traj_gen/observation/scene.py:Scene.other_text`, `traj_gen/prompts/state/human/scene.txt`, `state/other_person.txt`, `state/other_robot.txt`.
- **Person framing.** The `participant` task: walk to the goal station as one would in this study, with the other participant and the robot in the room, plus one physical statement of size: the robot is a disk of its prompt radius, the other participant about a disk of the person prompt radius, and the person cannot walk through either. It gives no instruction to give way or be cautious. The study context (a participant carrying blocks between stations) is in the system prompt. *Source:* the user's decision for the full pilot. *Code:* `traj_gen/prompts/framing/human/participant.txt`, `system/human/default.txt`, `traj_gen/observation/prompting.py:FRAMINGS`, `PromptBuilder.first`.
- **Prompt files.** `traj_gen/prompts/<part or harness setting>/[<kind>/]<option>.txt`, with a `human/` or `robot/` folder only where the text differs by the planned agent's kind; a part with one text per kind and no options is `<part>/<kind>.txt` (`plan_check/`). Texts that differ by kind only in who is speaking (the follow-up turns, the waypoint and pixel output texts, the other person's state line) are one template filled with the planned agent's words. *Code:* `traj_gen/observation/__init__.py:load_prompt`, `PLANNED_AGENT_WORDS`.
- **Positions and velocities the model reads.** Every real position in the text and on the map (the other agents' current positions and 2.8 s histories at 0.4 s spacing, and the planned agent's own history before it sets off) is read from the trip's 10 Hz series (`input_tracks_10hz`). Each turn also gives each other agent's last 0.4 s at 10 Hz: the three series positions 0.3, 0.2 and 0.1 s before now, between its last history point and its current position, so the shape of its latest motion is visible (user, 2026-11-23: "ok, then, yes, lets do 10 Hz"); the replay used for scoring, collisions, the recorded futures and the plan check keeps the scored tracks. The other agents' velocity is a central difference over the series samples 0.1 s before and after the current time (one-sided at the ends of the series), so it draws on one sample 0.1 s ahead. *Source:* user decision (2026-11-23: "use a central difference"). *Code:* `traj_gen/observation/scene.py:Scene`, `traj_gen/harness/episodes.py:Episode`.
- **The planned agent's own heading and speed.** Every turn, in every output mode, the planned agent's part of the state gives its speed and heading, in the units and with the heading convention of the steer turns (0 along the first axis, 90 along the second); the first turn explains the convention. They are the heading and speed at the end of the last executed step: for `steer` the heading the reply turned to (a turn while standing still included) and its distance per step, for `steer_vel` the end heading and speed of the arc, for positions and velocities the last executed step's direction (the last movement of at least 5 mm) and length. Before the agent sets off they come from its real history, backward only (its last 0.4 s step), since its future is what is generated. The steer modes plan from this same heading. *Source:* user decision (2026-11-24: "yes"). *Code:* `traj_gen/observation/scene.py:track_motion`, `Scene.text`, `traj_gen/outputs/planner.py:Planner.motion`, `traj_gen/outputs/steer.py:SteerPlanner.motion`, `traj_gen/harness/episodes.py:RollingEpisode`.
- **Later turns of a conversation.** A later turn repeats the whole state block but leaves out what the first turn already said: the map's legend (one caption line, "same legend as before"), the note on what the others' goals mean and the robot's size. Its opening line says only how long the previous plan was followed (the slowed-down note is in the system prompt). *Source:* user decision (2026-11-24: "I support them"). *Code:* `traj_gen/observation/prompting.py:PromptBuilder.followup`, `traj_gen/observation/scene.py:Scene.text`, `traj_gen/prompts/followup/default.txt`, `images/bev_followup.txt`.
- **Earlier reasoning stays in the conversation.** Each accepted reply enters the history with its reasoning, for both backends. Claude: the response's whole content, thinking blocks included and unchanged, as the Messages API requires for a later turn to read them; it reads them only while the conversation before them is unchanged, which holds since the conversation is only appended to. Qwen through vLLM: the reasoning in the assistant message's `reasoning` field, which vLLM passes to the chat template as `reasoning_content`, with the template's `preserve_thinking` on so every earlier reply keeps its think block (off, the template drops the think blocks of replies before the last user turn). The prompt grows by each turn's thinking. *Source:* user decision (2026-11-24: "did I not tell you that is what I want"). *Code:* `traj_gen/harness/clients.py:Chat.accept`, `QwenClient._api_messages`, `ClaudeClient._api_messages`.
- **Limitation.** Both generators are given the others' goals. A real robot would not know the people's goals, and Bi³ participants did not know each other's next station.

### Prompt radius versus scoring radius
- **Definition.** Prompts and model maps use larger disks than scoring: the robot's circumscribed disk (PR2 0.472 m, Stretch 0.237 m) and a 0.25 m person (a typical shoulder half-width). Scoring uses the body radii of Section 2. *Source:* design choice: planned paths kept colliding, so the prompts are deliberately stricter than the metrics. *Code:* `traj_gen/agents.py:prompt_radius`, `PERSON_PROMPT_RADIUS_M`.

### Speed caps
- **Robot, 0.3 m/s.** *Source:* measured. The real controllers' top speed in a trip has a median of 0.285 m/s (LAAS) and 0.291 m/s (UM) over the 144 robot trips, so 0.3 m/s is close to the top speed the real robots reach. *Code:* `traj_gen/agents.py:SPEED_CAP_M_S`.
- **Person, 1.5 m/s, one cap for every person.** *Source:* measured: the pooled 99th percentile of real per-step speed (0.4 s steps, raw mocap) over goal-to-goal moves of both sites is 1.501 m/s (LAAS 1.358, UM 1.603). 0.17% (LAAS) and 2.0% (UM) of real steps exceed it. Higher percentiles are not used because UM's tail above 2 m/s is inferred to be mocap jumps: it reaches 4.5 m/s (0.26% of steps), which no one walks in a 3.5 m room.
- **How a cap acts.** A plan is walked at the cap along its own shape, so a too-fast plan falls behind its timing. The plan's raw speeds before limiting give `speed_violation_rate` and `raw_speed_max`, and any step above the cap makes the episode invalid. *Code:* `traj_gen/outputs/planner.py:retime`.

### Output modes and episode types
- **Output modes.** Positions in the world frame (one point per step), steps from the current heading (`steer`, below), a velocity sequence, and a single velocity step. A plan has at most 75 steps (30 s). The positions output has one text for both kinds, filled with the planned agent's words (`traj_gen/prompts/output/positions.txt`). *Code:* `traj_gen/outputs/positions.py:PositionPlanner`, `traj_gen/outputs/velocity.py:VelocityPlanner`, `StepVelocityPlanner`, `traj_gen/outputs/planner.py:MAX_PLAN_STEPS`.
- **Units.** A harness setting, not a property of the output mode: `px` (pixels of the map, u right and v down) or `m` (metres of the room frame, x right and y up). Every number the model reads or writes goes through it: positions through the map frame, velocities through its axes, lengths and speeds through its scale, and the axis words, the grid labels of the map and the sense of a positive steer turn (from the first axis toward the second: clockwise on the map in pixels, anticlockwise in metres). A reply is converted back to metres by the same object, and everything inside the code is metres. `px` needs a map. *Source:* user decision (2026-11-23: "I think the unit is orthogonal largely"). *Code:* `traj_gen/observation/units.py`, `traj_gen/outputs/planner.py:UNITS`.
- **Episode types.** Single shot (one query at the start, the whole plan executed), rolling (re-query every 0.8 s of executed motion in a fresh chat for positions, `steer` and `steer_vel`, every 0.4 s for the single velocity step and the skills, and every 2 s for the velocity sequence; user, 2026-11-24, choosing between 0.8 s and 1.2 s, with 0.8 s set for now: `traj_gen/harness/presets.py:DEFAULT_EXECUTE_S`), and conversation (the same in one growing chat, `rolling_conv`). An episode ends at the first executed step within the arrival radius, or when the replay ends. *Code:* `traj_gen/harness/episodes.py`, `traj_gen/harness/presets.py:EPISODES`.
- **The steer harness.** One output mode with its own prompts, used only with the `scored_distances` (person, the primary use) and `robot_scored_distances` (robot) framings. It replaces the simple setup's prompts as a whole:
  - the task gives the scored contact distance and personal-space distance (from the body radii, not the prompt radii), says contact must not happen, and says the agent may wait in place;
  - the reply is one step per 0.4 s, `[turn, distance]` from the current heading, which each turn states, with the distance in the units. A turn is cut to 39° for the robot (the real robots' largest body yaw change per step within a trip: 39.0° at LAAS, 35.7° at UM, over the 144 robot trips of `trips_v3`) and 131° for a person (the 99th percentile of real walking-direction change between consecutive steps both above 0.2 m/s: 131.5° over the 14,292 trips of `trips_v3_human`), each rounded down to a whole degree, and a distance to one step's travel at the speed cap;
  - the robot's prompt states its largest distance per step (0.12 m, 13 px on the 448 px map). A person's gives only the top speed: with the stated maximum, people asked for exactly it in 75-82% of steps (run 20261117_human_m4a_f2b3123). The example reply is at each kind's scale;
  - the Qwen thinking budget is 32k tokens instead of 16k, since most calls with these prompts ran past 16k.

  *Source:* the robot test runs of 2026-11-16 and 2026-11-17 and the person runs of 2026-11-17 and 2026-11-23 (records in the effort's scratchpad). *Code:* `traj_gen/outputs/steer.py:SteerPlanner`, `traj_gen/prompts/framing/*/*scored_distances.txt`, `system/*/steer.txt`, `output/*/steer.txt`.
- **Steps as arcs (`steer_vel`).** The same harness with each step `[turn rate, speed]` instead of `[turn, distance]`: both are held for the step, so the agent follows a circular arc of radius speed / turn rate (the unicycle model), straight at a turn rate of 0, and each step starts from the heading the previous one ended with. The turn rate is cut to the kind's steer turn cap per step divided by 0.4 s, the speed to the kind's speed cap. It uses steer's framings and its system texts, has its own output texts (`traj_gen/prompts/output/<kind>/steer_vel.txt`) and no preset (`--overrides output=steer_vel`). *Source:* user decision (2026-11-23: "This makes is a properly interesting alternative"). *Code:* `traj_gen/outputs/steer.py:SteerVelocityPlanner`.
- **Skills (`skills`), a planned person only.** Every 0.4 s, in the rolling conversation, the model chooses one skill and code turns the choice into motion at the 10 Hz of the input series. The walker avoids other agents only through the chosen skill. *Source:* the skill design agreed with the user on 2026-11-23 and 2026-11-24 (the effort's design record), user: "Otherwise it isn't really just he model selecting and doesn't give the model a way to say \"be purely greedy\" or \"sit completely still\"". *Code:* `traj_gen/outputs/skills.py:SkillPlanner`, `traj_gen/prompts/system/human/skills.txt`, `output/human/skills.txt`, `reply/skills.txt`, `followup/skills.txt`, `skill_options/`.
  - **The skills.** Walk: a straight line to the goal at the chosen speed, ignoring everyone, stopping at the goal. Pass: toward the goal at the chosen speed under social force from one named agent, on a chosen side, with the walls repelling, until another skill is chosen. Wait: stop at once. Continue (offered from the second turn): keep the skill, agent, side, speed and clearance. A switch may jump in speed, with no smoothing (user: "within a given method we should use whatever is typical"). Speeds and clearances are in the harness unit; a speed above the person speed cap is cut to it, and a pass or walk speed must be above 0.
  - **The pass force** is PySocialForce 1.1.2 (MIT, a dependency): its goal force and wall force as they are, and its pedestrian force (Moussaid et al. 2009, Proc. R. Soc. B 276:2755) applied only to the agent being passed, through a subclass with three edits. The turning term's side is the chosen side instead of `-sign(theta)`; the distance d becomes d - delta for the clearance; and theta is wrapped to (-pi, pi], because the package's unwrapped angle difference gives no force at all for some approach directions (measured: a pair heading +x with the agent at (3, -0.05) m moving (-1, -0.1) m/s gets (0, 0), the same scene rotated 90 degrees gets 1.23 m/s^2). Parameters: the package's `DefaultConfig` for the goal force (relaxation time 0.5 s) and the pedestrian force (factor 5.1, lambda 2.0, gamma 0.35, n 2, n' 3), and the wall force's code defaults (factor 1, sigma 0.2 m, threshold 0.2 m beyond the walker's 0.15 m body). The `DefaultConfig` wall setting (factor 10, threshold 3.0 m) pushes 27.3 m/s^2 at a station's standing point 0.29 m from the wall, ten times the goal force, so no pass could arrive. Walls sit at the out-of-room boundary (room edge + 0.25 m). The walker's speed in a pass is capped at 1.3 times the chosen speed (the package's `max_speed_multiplier`, Helbing's v_max = 1.3 v0) and at 1.5 m/s; the desired speed is the chosen one. *Source:* user decision (2026-11-24: "Would it just be better to add the dependency"); the wrap fix, the parameter sets and the cap: the coordinator, 2026-11-24.
  - **Inside a decision.** Four 0.1 s sub-steps. Each reads the agent being passed from the trip's 10 Hz input series at that time (position, and the observation's central-difference velocity), never from the future positions in the text, so the true-future condition changes only what the model reads. The walker is integrated as the package's `PedState.step` does (velocity, then position), by our own loop, since `Simulator.step` zeroes the velocity within 0.5 m of the goal and would integrate the replayed agent. The walker's 10 Hz velocity carries over between decisions; the first decision starts from the heading and speed the state gives. The executed position is the one after the fourth sub-step. *Source:* user (2026-11-24: "the skill can use the higher-frequency ... This also aligns with how skills are finer-grained than the model").
  - **Sides.** Per other agent, "cross in front of / behind" when it moves at 0.15 m/s or more within 45 to 135 degrees of the walker's direction to its goal, otherwise "keep it on your left / right" (user: "ok, this seems reasonable"). Left gives turning side +1, right -1, in front -sign(cross(d, v)), behind +sign(cross(d, v)), with d the walker's direction to its goal and v the agent's velocity; in front and behind are re-read every sub-step while the agent moves at 0.15 m/s or more. A side is not offered when the distance from the agent's centre to the wall along the side's direction is below the pair's contact distance + 0.15 m (user: "your simple check makes sense").
  - **Clearance.** Chosen as a centre distance, raised to the pair's scored contact distance (robot 0.48 m at LAAS, 0.32 m at UM, person 0.30 m) when below it. delta = chosen clearance - contact distance.
  - **Records.** Per decision in plans.jsonl: the options offered, the reply, skill, agent, side word, the resolved turning side, speed, clearance and whether it was raised, delta, whether it continued or switched, and the four 10 Hz positions. Per episode in the manifest: decisions, switches, and per pass its times, agent, side, clearance, delta and closest centre distance at 10 Hz.
  - **Harness.** `--overrides output=skills` on `harness_v2` (no preset): only with `rolling_conv`, 0.4 s per decision, no plan check, and the scored-distance framing, whose wait sentence holds for skills as written.
- **Harness levels.** `rh_steer_px_bev_conv` is the steer harness. `rh_steer_px_bev_conv_true_future` (`prediction=true_future`) adds the other agents' recorded future positions in the text each turn, a proxy for a forecaster: the next 4 s when a person is planned and the next 12 s when the robot is (about the time the robot needs to cross the 3.5 m room at its 0.3 m/s cap), not the rest of the replay (user, 2026-11-23: "sounds good"; `traj_gen/outputs/planner.py:TRUE_FUTURE_S`). The plan check still tests against the whole recorded futures. `rh_steer_px_bev_conv_true_future_check` (`check=2`) adds the plan check: each plan is simulated against the recorded futures of every other agent, the first step in contact or inside personal space is reported back with the totals, and the model revises up to twice. The plan with the fewest contact steps is executed. Each pair is checked at its scored distances (`traj_gen/agents.py:pair_distances`, the same rule as `clearance_metrics`): a planned robot against the people at robot-person contact and personal space; a planned person against the robot at robot-person contact only, since the robot has no personal space, and against the other participant at 0.30 m contact and 0.40 m personal space, the distances the `scored_distances` task states. A step counts as contact if any pair is in contact, else as personal space; the reported agent is the one in contact first, then the nearest. The plan check gives the model the scored objectives, so it measures what the model can do with them, not unaided planning. *Code:* `traj_gen/harness/experiments.py` (`true_future`, `check_rounds`), `traj_gen/outputs/planner.py:QueryPlanner.extra_text`, `traj_gen/harness/episodes.py:Episode.revise`.

## 5. Known limitations
- **Replayed agents cannot react.** The other agents follow their logs, so they do not respond to the planned agent, and a real controller's replay is flattered by the people having reacted to it. The straight line is the fair baseline. This is a stated limitation, not something a planner is run to fix (a project decision).
- **Arrival multimodality.** One centre per station (Section 2) makes the arrival check lenient if arrivals cluster on separate sides of a station.
- **Robot and site are confounded.** Robot morphology and culture differ together across the two sites, so site differences cannot be attributed to either. *Source:* Bi³ paper, Sec. VII.
- **Disk bodies.** Contacts are measured between circles, so a contact is not a measured collision, and the robot's corners are not modelled.
- **Exploratory scoring.** The metrics, radii and thresholds here are exploratory, not validated against the Bi³ ratings.
- **Tables are not scored.** Stations are points, and passing through a table is neither modelled nor scored. The robot prompt still asks the model not to pass through stations, as general guidance.
